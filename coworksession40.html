Do not sell Cowork. Sell the management of Cowork.

Forty minutes is exactly enough time to make that sentence stick — if you leave with the lineup in your head, the reframe on your tongue, a real number on paper, and a named customer on your index card.

1. Purpose & Outcomes

Forty minutes is not enough time to teach Cowork. It's enough time to give you the four moves you need to have the conversation — and this design is built around exactly those four, in order. This guide picks up exactly where the Shadow AI discovery conversation leaves off: your customer has a Copilot license, or is about to, and now you have to decide what to say about Cowork.

By the end, you can

(a) explain Cowork's place in the customer conversation without a demo, (b) run the co-sell talk track against a Claude or ChatGPT preference, (c) size Cowork consumption for a customer's monthly bill on paper, and (d) name the two follow-on SOWs you'll pursue after any Cowork sale.

Everything below is a building block, deliberately. Each stop in Section 4 is independently reusable in a one-on-one customer meeting, a lunch-and-learn, or a QBR — if you only retain Stop 4 (the reframe) or Stop 5 (the cost story), you still walk out able to run half the conversation.

3. The Shape of the Session

Three acts in forty minutes — each one a building block you can redeploy on its own. The pacing is intentionally front-loaded on objection handling because that's where deals actually die: not in the pitch, in the second question from the CFO or the CIO who already has a Claude subscription.

1
Place it on the map
Stops 1–3 · Min 0–18

Where Cowork sits relative to Copilot Chat and the M365 Copilot seat, and why the Shadow AI conversation had to come first. No pitch yet — just the mental model the rest of the session hangs from.

2
Survive the two objections that kill the deal
Stops 4–5 · Min 18–32

“We already use Claude” and “this is going to cost a fortune.” Both get a reframe and a number, not a slogan.

3
Turn it into pipeline
Stops 6–7 · Min 32–40

Not a wrap-up — a commitment. You leave with a named account, a booked call, and the two SOWs you'll pitch next.

4. The Conversation, Beat by Beat

Every stop below has the same three-part anatomy: what you say (the talk track), what you show (the visual or comparison), and the segue — the exact line that pulls the conversation into the next stop. Read it top to bottom once before your first live run; after that, the segues alone are enough to keep you on track.

1

Opening Provocation

0:00–0:04

This is a brand-new opportunity — the customer isn't running Cowork yet. So don't open with someone else's usage report; open with the question that actually got you in the room.

What you say
Pick whichever of these already sounds like the meeting you're walking into — one of the three almost always fits.
“Don't we already have this with Claude?”

Their team already runs Claude — sanctioned or shadow — and someone just spotted “Cowork” surface inside Microsoft 365 Copilot.

You say“Fair question, and it's exactly the one this conversation answers. Same-caliber model, different governance. Let's talk about what changes when it runs inside your Microsoft 365 tenant instead of a personal subscription.”
“We saw this demoed at a conference and want it.”

An exec or your IT lead sat through a Cowork demo at an industry event or a Microsoft partner conference, walked away excited, and wants it running before anyone's checked the cost or whether their data's ready.

You say“Love that energy — let's turn it into a plan instead of a rush. Cowork bills differently than the seat you're used to, and turning it on before your data's ready just means you pay full price for output nobody trusts.”
“One person tried it and now leadership wants everyone on it.”

An early adopter ran a Cowork task, it impressed a VP, and now there's executive pressure to roll it out with no plan for cost or governance.

You say“That's the best kind of pressure to have — but ‘give everyone access’ is how a $30 seat turns into a runaway bill overnight. Let's build the rollout that keeps the magic and controls the meter.”
What you show
Nothing borrowed — the prospect's own numbers. If you already ran the Shadow AI discovery conversation with this account, this is where its findings earn their keep: pull up their Free Copilot MAU and Users Experiencing Usage Limits from ASPX, or the ChatGPT/Claude usage Defender for Cloud Apps already discovered. The point isn't a scary stat — it's proving you're talking about their tenant, not a generic pitch.
→
Segue: “Whichever one of those just described your meeting — the next thirty-six minutes gives you the same four moves. So before we talk about what Cowork does, let's agree on where it sits — because pitching it like a bigger Copilot Chat is the fastest way to lose the thread.”

The Shadow AI Wedge

0:12–0:18
What you say
One sentence, said slowly: “Governance readiness is consumption readiness.” Cowork grounds in Microsoft 365 data through Work IQ and honors Purview sensitivity labels — a poorly governed estate yields unreliable Cowork output at the same credit cost. If you already ran the Shadow AI discovery conversation with this account, this is a callback: “Remember the oversharing finding from this morning — three ‘Confidential’ sites shared with everyone? Cowork will happily pull from those the same way Copilot Chat would. The credits get spent either way; only the output quality changes.”
What you show
The Purview support snapshot — what Cowork governs today vs. not yet, so you don't overclaim:

Sensitivity label inheritance on outputs · audit logging of prompts/responses · eDiscovery on Cowork content · Data Lifecycle Management & retention · Communication Compliance policies

Not yet — say this honestly

DLP specific to Cowork (Microsoft lists it “coming soon”) · Cowork interactions in the Apps & Agents dashboard · Cowork interactions in the AI observability page

Segue: “Now — the objection you're all thinking about, because every one of your customers already has an AI subscription that isn't Microsoft's.”
4

“Same Model, Different Governance”

0:18–0:26

The highest-leverage eight minutes in the session.

What you say
Say the line, then justify it, then drill it once with a colleague if you have time to rehearse (full drill in Section 9).
“Keep Claude. Bring it under management. Cowork is what runs the work your Microsoft 365 tenant already knows about.”The line to use three times per Cowork conversation
What you show
Two facts to keep straight, so you don't over-promise on model quality:
The two facts

The underlying model is the same family — Copilot Cowork runs Anthropic Opus 4.8 and Sonnet 4.6 at GA, with GPT 5.5 in Frontier and a lower-cost Cowork 1 model coming. Don't argue “our model is better” — you'll lose that argument. Instead: Microsoft's own disclosed internal testing showed Copilot Cowork averaging 30–40% cheaper per prompt than Claude Cowork with its Microsoft 365 connector, on the same model, same tasks (125 runs, 12 prompts, both on Opus 4.8, June 2026).

Personal Claude / ChatGPT subscription

Outside the trust boundary. No Purview labels, no Defender for Cloud Apps visibility, no Cost Management, no Entra identity binding. The model may be excellent — the operations around it don't exist.

Copilot Cowork

Inside the Microsoft 365 trust boundary. Purview, Defender for Cloud Apps, Entra, and Cost Management all apply automatically. Don't out-argue model quality — out-argue enterprise operations.

Want the full dimension-by-dimension teardown (billing, regional constraints, partner monetization)? That's Section 5 — keep it in your back pocket for the CIO who wants receipts.

Rehearsed objection handles

“Claude is a better model.”

Same model family under both. Cowork's edge isn't the model — it's what wraps it: governance, cost controls, and a tenant you already manage.

“Claude Cowork is cheaper.”

Microsoft's disclosed internal testing showed Copilot Cowork 30–40% cheaper per prompt on the same model, same tasks. Offer to run the comparison live.

“We just want the desktop app.”

Fine for one person. Ask who manages spend, audits prompts, and enforces DLP across the team — that's the tenant story only Copilot Cowork has.

“Anthropic has SOC 2 / ISO certs too.”

True — but Cowork activity isn't yet captured in Anthropic's own audit logs or Compliance API. Purview captures it today, inside your existing estate.

“It's simpler — one subscription.”

Simpler until spend needs a cap, an owner, or an auditor. That's exactly the governance retainer the partner is built to run.

Where you must not overstate

Don't claim Cowork is universally cheaper — the 30–40% figure is specific to Microsoft's tested prompt set.

Don't claim DLP is available for Cowork today — it's “coming soon.”

Don't claim Cowork appears in every Purview surface today — Apps & Agents and AI observability don't show it yet.

Don't sell Cowork into EU/EFTA/UK/gov-cloud tenants without checking Anthropic sub-processor status.

Don't sell Cowork before the customer has a CSP-channel Azure subscription — they'll hit the billing wall.

6. The Licensing Snapshot — What to Quote Without Blinking

Stop 6's objection “we don't own Purview or Defender for Cloud Apps” only lands cleanly if you have the current SMB licensing math memorized. Here it is.

$22
Business Premium, per user/month, paid yearly (2026 pricing)
~$15
Combined Defender + Purview Suite add-on for Business Premium, per user/month — the E5-capability unlock without the E5 forklift
50%
Promotional discount on Purview Suite when purchased alongside Microsoft 365 Copilot
Wrong pitch
“You need to upgrade to E5.”

Business Premium customers hear this as “double my Microsoft bill” — deal stalls.

Right pitch
Same Copilot engine, right stack

“The Copilot AI engine is identical on Business Premium and E5” — Microsoft states this plainly. The difference is the governance stack in the base SKU, not the Copilot feature set.

SMB-native path
BP + Copilot + Purview Suite

Business Premium + Copilot + Purview Suite add-on + optional Defender Suite add-on. Same Copilot experience, right governance stack, no E5 forklift.

Verify before quoting
The DSPM for AI catch

DSPM for AI ships in the Purview Suite for Business Premium add-on for third-party AI monitoring, but full Copilot data assessments may still require E5 Compliance in some configurations. Confirm against current licensing tables before quoting.

This is exactly what the License Uplift Calculator (§2) is built to walk a customer through line by line — three columns (BP alone / BP + Suites / E5), each Shadow AI capability marked ✓ / partial / ✗, so nobody has to take the delta on faith.

8. The Eight Discovery Questions You Walk In With

These are the bridge from this conversation to your first customer call. Better still: don't start from a blank slate. Pull your top account through ASPX first (§2) so Question 8 already has a data point behind it before the customer even answers.

  1. Which AI tools does the leadership team know your staff is using?
  2. Which AI tools does your staff actually use? (It's more than the answer to Q1.)
  3. What data classification standard exists today — what labels, and who owns them?
  4. Do you have an AI Acceptable Use Policy? When was it last reviewed?
  5. Is there an approved Copilot budget, and is Cowork line-itemed separately from it?
  6. What's your cyber insurance renewal date — does the questionnaire ask about AI use?
  7. What does your SharePoint estate look like — Copilot-ready, or an oversharing risk?
  8. If you named your top three AI use cases, what would they be? (The Cowork positioning wedge — chat can't do them; Cowork can.)

9. If You Have 90 Minutes Instead of 40

The forty-minute design is deliberately spare. When the agenda allows a longer block, these five exercises turn the same building blocks into hands-on practice.

  1. Simulate your own customer. Open the Cost Simulator (§2), enter a real target account's persona mix, model a pre-purchase discount, and print a co-branded leave-behind.
  2. Design your Cowork monthly retainer. Scorecard, artifacts, cadence, price point for a 25-seat customer — talk it through with a colleague or your manager.
  3. The billing-wall drill. Have a colleague play the customer's CFO while you walk the Azure plan purchase and Cost Management activation from memory.
  4. The Claude objection drill. Pair up: one of you plays a CIO who prefers Claude Cowork, the other reframes using Stop 4's script and the §5 deep comparison. Two rounds each, then switch.
  5. The month-one review dry run. Using a sample tenant's numbers, draft your month-two recommendation, then have a peer tear it apart.

10. Keep Your Delivery Sharp

  • ✓ Lead with the customer's own number — you have ASPX access, so pull it before Stop 1.
  • ✓ Say “same model, different governance” three times — Stop 4, again in Stop 5, once more in the close.
  • ✓ Say “sanction” in the same breath as “block,” every time. The posture is discover → sanction → govern, not shutdown.
  • ✓ Protect the clock in Stop 4, not Stop 2. The lineup card can compress to ninety seconds if things are moving fast; the objection reframe needs its full eight minutes.
  • ✓ Close with the written commitment, not open Q&A. Save questions for after — write your card, say it out loud, and move.
  • ✗ Do not demo Cowork live. This is a strategic conversation, not a lab — if you must show anything, it's the screenshot, not a live tenant.
  • ✗ Do not sell Cowork into EU/EFTA/UK/gov-cloud tenants without a caveat — check Anthropic sub-processor status first.
  • ✗ Do not plan to sell Cowork before confirming a CSP-channel Azure subscription exists — you'll hit the billing wall in week one.

The three revenue shapes to keep in your head
