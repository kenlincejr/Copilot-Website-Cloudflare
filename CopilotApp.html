Part One · Choosing Your Copilot
Choosing the Right Copilot

Everyone reads this. Which Copilot you have, where it lives, and which one to open.

Section 1

The 30-Second Answer

Microsoft ships several things called Copilot. Before choosing between them, it is worth knowing that the first question is not which one should I open — it is which one am I entitled to. That single fact determines whether the rest of this guide applies to you at all.

If this is you…You have…And it can see…
You signed in with a personal accountConsumer CopilotThe public web. None of your work data.
You signed in for work, no Copilot add-onCopilot ChatThe web, plus files you paste or upload into the chat yourself.
Your organization bought you the add-onMicrosoft Copilot (licensed)Your email, files, meetings and chats — everything your account can already open.
■ And the one rule that follows
If you have the license, the control that matters most is Work IQ. Turn it on when the answer should come from your organization's data. Turn it off when you want the open web. Everything else in Part One is refinement on top of those two facts.

Section 2

Which Copilot Do You Actually Have?

This is the question the rest of the guide depends on, and the one most often answered wrongly. The three products share a name, share an icon family, and increasingly share a front door. They do not share what they can see.

Consumer Copilot
Free, personal account.
Lives at copilot.microsoft.com.

Good for general questions and personal tasks. It cannot see anything belonging to your employer, and you should not paste work content into it.
Copilot Chat
Included with Microsoft 365.
Work account, no add-on required.

Web-grounded chat with enterprise data protection, Copilot Pages, image generation and file upload. It does not reach into your mailbox or SharePoint on its own.
Microsoft Copilot (licensed)
The paid add-on.
Lives at m365.cloud.microsoft.

Everything above, plus grounding in your Microsoft 365 data, in-app Copilot across Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams, Copilot Search, Notebooks, and the Researcher and Analyst agents.
⚠ How to tell in ten seconds
Open Copilot and ask it something only your organization would know — "what did I miss in my meetings yesterday?" or "find the last document I edited."

If it answers from your actual work, you have the license. If it explains that it cannot reach your files, you are on Copilot Chat. If it never asked you to sign in with a work account at all, you are on the consumer app.

The other reliable tell is the presence of the Work IQ control. It only appears where work grounding is available.
⚠ Why this matters more than it sounds
Most "Copilot doesn't work" complaints are entitlement problems wearing a technical costume. A user on Copilot Chat follows a guide written for the licensed product, asks it to summarize their inbox, gets a polite refusal, and concludes the product is broken.

Nothing was broken. They were never entitled to that capability. Establish the tier before troubleshooting anything else.

Section 3

The Names Changed — Reading the Branding

Until recently this guide told you to look for the "365" in the name. That test no longer works. Microsoft has been dropping "365" from the branding, and its own documentation now uses Microsoft Copilot for both the licensed work product and the free consumer app. The name alone will not tell you which one you are in.

Two things still will: the account you signed in with, and the address it lives at.

Not this one
copilot.microsoft.com

The consumer assistant. A personal account signs in here. It is not connected to your organization's data and never sees your email, files or meetings. Fine for personal tasks — but do not paste work content into it.

✓ This one
m365.cloud.microsoft

The work assistant. Your Microsoft Entra work or school account signs in here, and this is the surface described throughout this guide. Copilot Chat lives at m365copilot.com and redirects into the same experience — what differs is your license, not the address.

⚠ On a work device you may be asked to choose
Opening the consumer Copilot with a managed account can prompt you to pick work or personal. Choosing work redirects you into the licensed experience and shows what your account can reach. Choosing personal signs you into the consumer product with no access to organizational data.

If you are ever unsure which you landed in, apply the ten-second test in Section 2.

Section 4

Work IQ — The One Control That Changes Every Answer

In August 2026 Microsoft merged what had been two separate chat experiences — web chat and work chat — into a single surface, with one toggle governing where answers come from. If you learned Copilot before that change, this is the most important thing to re-learn.

✓ Work IQ ON
Copilot answers from your organization's data — the email, files, chats, meetings and SharePoint content your own account can already open.

Use it for: catching up, finding things, summarizing — anything where the answer already exists inside your tenant and you just need it surfaced.
■ Work IQ OFF
Copilot draws on the open web as well as what your account can reach.

Use it for: research, current events, general knowledge, drafting from scratch — anything where your tenant is not the authority.
■ The mistake to design against
A disappointing answer is very often a grounding problem rather than a prompt problem. Asking "what are the current licensing options?" with Work IQ on returns whatever your tenant happens to hold — possibly a two-year-old deck someone saved to SharePoint. The same question with Work IQ off reaches current public documentation.

Before rewriting a prompt that let you down, check which way the toggle was set. That fixes more bad answers than better prompting does.

This control only exists where work grounding is possible, which makes it the quickest way to confirm which Copilot you are in — see Section 2.

Section 5

Where Copilot Lives — The Surface Map

Copilot is not one place. Once entitlement and grounding are settled, the remaining question is where to invoke it — and the honest answer is that the surface matters less than it once did, because they share an orchestrator and a permission model. What still differs is how much context arrives for free.

SurfaceReach for it whenNeeds the license?
Copilot in TeamsWork is happening right now — a live meeting, an active thread. Context arrives free.Yes
The Copilot app (web or desktop)You are orienting rather than executing — exploring, planning, searching broadly.No — Chat tier works
Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OutlookThe artifact is the point. Drafting, editing and analyzing in place.Yes, for full editing
Copilot SearchYou want to find and scan source material yourself rather than read a synthesis.Yes
Copilot NotebooksA project you return to repeatedly. Pin the sources once; stop re-explaining context.Yes
Copilot PagesThe output needs to be worked on with other people.No
Agents and the Agent StoreThe same shaped task recurs and you want it packaged.Partly — see below
■ Notebooks are the most under-used of these
If you find yourself starting a fresh chat every morning and pasting the same background in, that is a Notebook. Attach the relevant files, meetings and emails once, and every subsequent question is answered against that pinned set rather than against your whole tenant.

For recurring project work it is a better answer than the one this guide previously gave, which was simply "open the app and search."

Agents built only on instructions and public websites are free; agents that reach tenant data are metered and off by default for Copilot Chat users. The Copilot Agent Knowledge Reference covers how to build one and what each knowledge source can actually hold.

Section 6

Execution vs. Orientation — Teams or the App

The reason there are two Copilots isn't about features — both can answer questions, write, summarize, and brainstorm. The difference is what you're trying to do when you open them. Microsoft designed each one around a different mental state.

Copilot in Microsoft Teams

You're already in the middle of work.

Think of this as a smart teammate sitting next to you while a meeting is happening or a conversation is live. It already knows the chat thread, transcript, and attached files — and skips straight to helping you act.

Best for: Summarizing meetings · Drafting replies · Generating action items · Catching up on threads

The Copilot App

You're figuring out what to work on next.

Think of this as a quiet research room where nothing is assumed. You can search broadly across your email, files, meetings, and chats without already knowing exactly what you're looking for.

Best for: Finding documents · Researching across projects · Planning · Exploring before you dive in

Copilot in Teams helps you execute.
The Copilot app helps you orient.

A useful mental test: do you already know what task you're in? If yes, open Teams. If you're still figuring out what needs to happen, open the Copilot app.

■ The Execution vs. Orientation Test
"Am I doing something right now — or figuring out what to do?"

Doing something (in a meeting, replying to a thread, reviewing a document) → Use Copilot in Teams.
Figuring out what to do (finding a file, preparing for a project, planning next steps) → Use the Copilot app.

Section 7

Teams Meetings — What Copilot Can Actually See

Meeting Copilot is the most common source of "it worked last week and now it doesn't." The behavior is not random. It is governed by a setting the meeting organiser chose, often without realising what it decided.

Organiser's Copilot settingDuring the meetingAfter it ends
On, with transcript savedWorksWorks — Recap and chat
Only during the meetingWorksGone entirely
OffUnavailableUnavailable
⚠ "Only during the meeting" is the one that surprises people
Under this setting Copilot works from live speech-to-text that is discarded when the meeting ends. Nothing is retained. Afterwards Copilot is not available in the meeting's Recap tab, and for calls it is not available in the chat thread or call history either.

From the attendee's point of view Copilot simply worked during the call and vanished after it. This is not a fault and there is no recovery — the data no longer exists. Organizations choose this setting deliberately, because it delivers meeting Copilot without creating a retained transcript.
■ What to do about it
If you need a recap afterwards, you need a saved transcript — and that is the organiser's decision, made when the meeting is set up, not something an attendee can change later.

If you are the organiser and post-meeting summaries matter to your team, choose a setting that saves the transcript. If your organization has standardized on "only during the meeting" for retention reasons, ask Copilot your questions before the meeting ends. That is the entire window.

This is separate from whether Copilot can see the surrounding chat, files and thread, which it can regardless. The transcript setting governs spoken content only.

Real-World Scenarios

Not sure which to open? Match your situation to one of these common cases.

You joined a 45-minute Teams meeting 20 minutes late

Ask "Catch me up" — if transcription is on, Copilot summarizes what you missed in seconds. If the organiser chose "only during the meeting," this works live and disappears afterwards. See Section 7.

Copilot in Teams

You need to find a proposal from last quarter

Search or chat across all your files and emails without knowing exactly where it was saved.

Copilot App

You want to draft a reply to a long channel thread

Copilot reads the thread and drafts a reply in context — no copy-pasting required.

Copilot in Teams

You're preparing for a client you haven't spoken to in months

Ask Copilot to surface recent emails, shared files, and meeting notes involving that client across all of M365.

Copilot App

You want action items from yesterday's standup

Copilot pulls the saved transcript and generates a structured action item list. This only works if the meeting was transcribed or recorded — otherwise there is nothing left to read.

Copilot in Teams

You don't know what you need — you just know there's something to figure out

Start here. The app's broader, exploratory design is built for open-ended starting points.

Copilot App

Section 10

One Orchestrator, Several Models

For a long time the honest answer here was "same brain, different assumptions" — one model, one orchestrator, one Graph, and only the pre-loaded context differing between surfaces. That is no longer accurate. The orchestration layer and the permission model are still shared. The model is not.

Since 2026 the underlying model is partly a user-visible choice. Anthropic models can be selected alongside OpenAI ones for some tasks, and Word's default moved to Sonnet 5 with a higher default reasoning level in August 2026. Two surfaces can now answer the same prompt with different models at different reasoning effort — so "it gave me a better answer in Word" is sometimes literally true rather than a perception.

What differs is the pre-prompt context — the information Copilot receives before it ever reads your message. This pre-loaded context shapes everything: how broadly Copilot searches, how quickly it synthesizes, and what it assumes you're trying to accomplish.

■ What Is Shared, and What Is Not
Orchestration: The same Copilot service layer routes and grounds every request
Permissions: Identical Microsoft Graph access — org security policy applies equally
Compliance: Sensitivity labels, DLP and Purview controls apply identically
Not shared — the model: selectable in some surfaces, and Word defaults to Sonnet 5 at a higher reasoning level
Not shared — the agents: Researcher and Analyst need a Copilot license; Copilot Chat alone does not include them

Section 11

How Context Is Loaded

Every Copilot interaction begins with a system prompt — a behind-the-scenes instruction set that tells the model who you are, what you have access to, and what it should prioritize. In Teams, that system prompt is automatically populated with rich contextual data from your current session. In the M365 app, it is not.

Teams Copilot — Context-Dense Mode

When you open Copilot inside a Teams chat, channel, or meeting, the orchestration layer pre-loads the following before processing your prompt:

  • The current conversation thread (messages, reactions, shared files)
  • The meeting transcript if a call is in progress or recently ended
  • Participant list and recent activity within the thread
  • A computed time window — Copilot determines what counts as 'recent'
  • Files attached or referenced within the active thread context

This dense pre-loading lets Copilot skip the discovery phase entirely. It already knows what's relevant. Retrieval is narrow and fast — optimized for synthesis speed over breadth.

Copilot App — Context-Expansive Mode

When you open the Copilot app, no session-specific context is pre-loaded. The system prompt gives Copilot access to your full M365 tenant data but does not narrow the scope. Copilot must infer relevance from your prompt alone.

  • No assumed conversation, meeting, or thread
  • Full Microsoft Graph scope: email, files, calendar, chats, SharePoint, Teams data
  • Discovery is part of the interaction — Copilot surfaces breadth before depth
  • Search mode is a first-class interface, not a secondary option
  • Results are diverse across content types before being synthesized

Section 12

Retrieval, the Graph, and What Copilot Can See

Microsoft 365 Copilot uses semantic search over the Microsoft Graph — an index of your organization's emails, chats, files, calendar events, and SharePoint content. The critical architectural difference between the two surfaces is how aggressively retrieval is scoped before your prompt is processed.

Retrieval Dimension Copilot in Teams Copilot App
Search scopeNarrow — current thread / meetingBroad — full Microsoft Graph
Source diversityLow — current context prioritizedHigh — mail, files, chats, calendar
Pre-filteringAggressive — time window + threadMinimal — relies on your prompt
Synthesis timingImmediateAfter exploration (on request)
Result surfaceComposed summary or draftSearch results + optional summary
Latency profileLower (smaller retrieval surface)Higher (broader search)
Best when you know…Exactly what task you're inNothing yet — still discovering

Microsoft Graph, and What Copilot Can Actually See

Microsoft Graph is the unified API powering Copilot's access to your organizational data — indexing Exchange, SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams, and other M365 services. Both Copilot surfaces query the same Graph with identical permissions. Security and compliance policies apply equally.

The difference is how the query is scoped: Teams Copilot appends thread-specific filters automatically; the Copilot app sends broader semantic queries and lets results determine scope.

⚠ Important — Data Access and Privacy
Neither Copilot surface grants access beyond your existing Microsoft 365 permissions. Copilot cannot surface files you don't have access to, emails not addressed to you, or content your admin has restricted. Sensitivity labels, DLP policies, and Microsoft Purview compliance controls apply in both apps equally.

Section 13

What Microsoft Doesn't Publish

This part of the guide describes internal behavior, and honesty about the boundary is what makes the rest of it trustworthy. Some of what follows is documented by Microsoft. Some is careful inference from observed behavior. They deserve to be told apart.

■ Documented, and safe to repeat Documented
That both surfaces share an orchestrator and a permission model. That Copilot cannot exceed a user's existing Microsoft 365 permissions. That sensitivity labels, DLP and Purview controls apply equally. That Work IQ governs grounding. That Researcher and Analyst require a license. That model selection is now partly user-visible.
⚠ Inference, and worth flagging as such Inference
Microsoft does not publish Copilot's system prompts, its chunking parameters, its retrieval ranking, or the exact context assembled before your message. Any description of a distinct "reasoning posture" per surface — including earlier editions of this guide, which stated it as fact — is a model built from observed behavior, not a specification.

The observation is real and useful: Teams Copilot does answer faster and more narrowly, and the standalone app does return broader, more exploratory results. The explanation is inference. Treat it as a working mental model that predicts behavior well, and do not quote it to a customer as though Microsoft had documented it.
■ Why this section exists
A guide that states everything with equal confidence gives you no way to tell which parts will survive contact with a skeptical customer. Marking the boundary costs a paragraph, and means the rest of the document can be relied on without hedging.

If a claim in this guide is not marked as inference, it is documented, and the source is listed in Section 15.

Section 14

Summary: The Design Philosophy

Each Copilot surface is purpose-built for a different cognitive mode: doing (executing a known task) or thinking (figuring out what to do next).

Design Dimension Copilot in Teams Copilot App
Assumed cognitive stateExecutionOrientation / Discovery
Assumed urgencyHighLow to moderate
Retrieval strategyNarrow and fastBroad and thorough
Synthesis timingImmediateOn demand
UI metaphorTeammate in the roomQuiet research library
Primary failure modeMisses outside-context infoCan feel slow for urgent tasks
Primary UX signalSpeed and completenessBreadth and control
One Copilot helps you decide. The other helps you deliver.
Both are powerful. The right choice depends on where you are in your work.
