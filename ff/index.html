The whole draft, computed while you're on the clock.

Draftline is a full draft room: 267 players scored in your league's rules, a live board that re-ranks itself as picks come off, odds on who survives to your next turn, eleven modeled opponents you can rehearse against, and a graded report when it's done.

Two minutes on the clock is not enough time to work any of that out in your head. It is plenty of time to read it off a screen.

There is no server behind this page and no account to make. A profile is a place in this browser to keep your draft, and the password only locks it on this device — a lock on a drawer, not a bank vault. Clearing site data deletes it, and the app has an export button for keeping a copy.

Nothing to install, nothing to sign up for, and no network call once you are drafting.

Paste your league settings

Copy the settings page out of Yahoo and paste it in. Draftline reads the scoring, the roster shape, the team count and the draft order out of it, and shows you what it parsed before anything is saved. Or start from a preset and edit the numbers.

Draft

Type a name, hit Enter — somebody took him. Shift+Enter means you did. Ctrl+Z undoes any depth. Every number on screen re-computes on each pick while the clock runs.

Read the report

Every roster in the league gets scored in your own rules, ranked, and graded on a percentile — computed, not asked for. Then Claude reads the table and names your biggest hole, the rival who is the real threat, and two moves to make.

What makes it different

Six things a free draft board does not do.

Your rules, not the consensus

Scored in your league's points

Everything else ranks by consensus ADP, which encodes somebody else's scoring. Draftline starts from a stat-level projection and re-scores it — receptions, 40-yard bonuses, return yards, your defensive tiers, your roster shape — and derives replacement level from your league instead of a hardcoded list.

Survival, not vibes

Who is actually still there at your turn

Every player carries a real draft-position standard deviation from about 7,800 mock drafts. Picking back-to-back at the turn, the board tells you who is likely to last one more pick — the difference between a reach and a free roll.

A room, not a ranking

Eleven opponents that draft like people

The modeled room draws near each player's own draft position rather than taking the top of a list, obeys roster limits against what every team has actually taken, and leans into a positional run once one starts. Reaches and slides happen at the rate they really happen.

Rehearse it

Run the whole night before it counts

Practice mode drafts the room out and stops the moment the pick is yours — same board, same suggestions, same clock. Or run 25 mock drafts forward from wherever you currently stand and see the median roster you end up holding. About 80 milliseconds for the lot.

Styles that show their work

Nine strategies, each with a receipt

Hero RB, Zero RB, RB-heavy and six more — each a set of real knobs on the same score, not a label. Before one applies you see which players move and by how many places, and every pick afterward carries a line saying what your style did to it.

On deck

A briefing waiting when your clock starts

Two picks out, Draftline asks Claude one question and puts the answer on screen before it is your turn. It gets the board's own numbers, the research notes on the players left, and the thing no ADP tool has: what the teams picking ahead of you still need.
