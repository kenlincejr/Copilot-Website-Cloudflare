## 1. The central insight

The Microsoft program ladder and the Customer Zero service ladder are **the same motion viewed from two sides**. Partners treat them as competing priorities — "do I chase points, or do I sell work?" — and that framing is the mistake. Every service in the house catalog ([customer-zero-starter-kit/build-sellable-services.md](../../customer-zero-starter-kit/build-sellable-services.md)) *emits* the exact evidence a program metric consumes.

The field guide's job is to make that a single ladder, not two.

| House service offering | Sells because | Program evidence it emits | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Copilot Exposure Assessment** | "We don't know what Copilot could expose" | CPOR association at first touch; net customer add (11–300-seat tenant) | Modern Work Performance (2 pts/customer); starts the usage-growth clock |
| **Shadow AI Discovery & Redirect** | "Users are already using AI outside IT's view" | Security net customer add; Defender/Purview workload association | Security Performance (SMB: 5 adds × 4 pts) |
| **Oversharing Remediation** | "We have old permissions, broad grants, stale guests" | Documented repeatable remediation runbook + before/after deltas | **Data Security specialization audit evidence**; Security Deployments metric |
| **Label Taxonomy & Training** | "Information protection is too complex" | Purview Information Protection deployment; audit-ready project artefacts | Security Deployments (6 net new); Data Security audit |
| **Copilot Adoption & Champion Enablement** | "Users have licenses but don't change how they work" | **Paid Copilot MAU growth** — the single performance metric of the M365 Copilot specialization | Modern Work Usage growth (SMB: 500 MAU via CPOR); **Copilot specialization Performance** |
| **Managed Copilot Governance** | "A remediated tenant will drift" | Retained MAU; 40%-active deployment threshold sustained | Modern Work Deployments (40% of paid licences active); renewal-proof MRR |
| **Governed Agent Starter** | "We want agents but need identity, lifecycle, support" | Copilot Studio customer deployment (≥$10K TTM counts) | **Agentic Business Solutions specialization** Pathway Two |

**The line for the guide:** you do not stop selling to go earn points. The points are a byproduct of selling the right five things in the right order — provided you claim CPOR on day one of every engagement, because usage growth counts only from the association date.

## 2. The roadmap, re-based for a partner who already has a distributor

Six phases. Phases are named for what the partner *achieves*, not what they file.

### Phase 0 — Baseline (week 1)

Not a build step; a measurement step. Most partners in this base have already accidentally accumulated points and have never looked.

- Partner Center → Insights → **Partner Capability Score**. Record the score in each solution area, broken into Performance / Skilling / Customer success.
- Partner Center → **Membership** → check which benefits package is active (if any) and its renewal date.
- List every customer tenant and its seat count. **Only 11–300-seat tenants count for Modern Work SMB; 5–300 paid licences on at least one workload for Security SMB.**
- Check existing **CPOR associations**. Most partners have near-zero and are leaving the cheapest points on the table.
- Distributor asset: TD SYNNEX **Partner Maturity Assessment** (AI, Azure, Data & Security baseline) and **MAICPP Clinics** (live PCS-scoring webinars) exist for exactly this step. [Repo-sourced: frontier.html Stage 0/1 resource lines]

**Exit test:** you can state your current PCS in Modern Work and Security to the point, and you know how many of your tenants are score-eligible.

### Phase 1 — Claim what you already earned (weeks 1–4)

The highest-ROI phase in the entire roadmap, and it costs nothing.

- **File CPOR claims on every workload you influence.** CPOR thresholds are a quarter to a half of the CSP thresholds for the same points, and Microsoft awards on whichever association type scores higher — so claiming both is free upside. Processing: up to 30 days from claim, plus a 90-day customer-consent window. Start now.
- Buy the right benefits package. For a Copilot practice, **Partner Success Expanded ($4,125/yr)** is the only one with M365 Copilot seats (10) plus a 25,000/mo Copilot Studio credit pack — it is the internal lab, the demo tenant, and the Customer Zero evidence base in one SKU. Core ($925) is the budget floor.
- Deploy internally. **Become Customer Zero before selling the motion** — the capture discipline in the starter kit is what later becomes specialization audit evidence.

**Exit test:** CPOR filed on every managed tenant; Copilot running inside your own firm with time-on-task and before/after deltas being captured.

### Phase 2 — First incentive dollars (months 1–6)

The gate here is **not** the designation. It is 25 capability points + $25K trailing-twelve-month CSP revenue at the PLA.

- Sell the SMB Copilot SKUs: Business Premium + Copilot ($32/user), Business Standard + Copilot ($23.50), Copilot Business ($21, promo $18 to 31 Dec 2026). **Copilot in 30** (25 users, 30 days, <300 employees, through 31 Dec 2026) is the purpose-built pilot container — a 30-day window with a defined readout and a day-30 conversion conversation, into which the Exposure Assessment fits as the pre-trial step.
- Certify the designation floor: 2 intermediate + 1 advanced on the Modern Work SMB path (~$495 of exams). Skilling points land ~10 days after a cert completes.
- Run the assess → remediate motion on 3–5 customers. Each one is a net customer add *and* a case study.

**Exit test:** MCI enrolled and earning (12.5% growth accelerator + 7% on Copilot/E5/E7/Agent 365/Copilot Studio, paid 60% cash / 40% co-op).

### Phase 3 — Designation (months 6–12, $4,875/yr)

- 70/100 points, every metric above zero, on **any single day** in a rolling six-month window. The "one good day" rule is materially generous and under-exploited: time the certification completions and the deployment claims to land in the same month.
- **Earn Modern Work and Security together.** One $4,875 fee covers every designation you qualify for. Security SMB needs only 5 net adds and three certified people (SC-500, SC-200, and one of SC-100/SC-300 — one person can hold two).
- Modern Work returns 200× E5, 20× Copilot seats, $4,000 Azure; Security adds $10,000 Azure and $5,000/yr Security Copilot credits; both add 50 support incidents and 50 advisory hours.

**Exit test:** designation purchased; you are now inside the gate for most partner-led incentives and for co-sell eligibility.

### Phase 4 — Copilot specialization (months 12–24)

The deadline phase. **From 1 January 2027, Copilot engagement incentives require this specialization** (Jumpstart Ready honored only to 31 Dec 2026).

- Performance: paid Copilot MAU only. Threshold unpublished post-July-2026 — **verify in Partner Center before budgeting**.
- Skilling: 5 individuals across SC-401, AB-100, AB-620.
- Evidence: partner-funded third-party capabilities audit, valid 2 years, requiring real customer examples.
- The audit validates *documented, repeatable delivery* — which is precisely the Customer Zero capture layer (time-on-task, decision logs, before/after deltas, anonymized proof). Build it once; it is simultaneously the audit artefact and the sellable deliverable.

**Exit test:** specialization held; Copilot incentive earning protected through the January 2027 cutover.

### Phase 5 — Compound or stop (months 24+)

Two honest options, and the guide should present them as equally respectable:

- **Stop at "practice complete":** Modern Work + Security designations, M365 Copilot + Data Security specializations. This is the profitable plateau for an SMB partner — ahead of nearly every competitor in the segment, with the full incentive stack and a governance annuity.
- **Continue to Frontier:** add a Cloud & AI Platforms designation and an Azure AI specialization. Multi-year, enterprise-shaped, and the badge itself retires June 2027 in favour of a harder specialization. Pursue only if the Azure practice is a genuine strategic goal, not for the badge.

## 3. What changes for a partner who is *already* with a distributor

Things dossiers 00–03 treat as steps, this reader has already done — and things they should *not* redo:

| Already done | Note |
|---|---|
| MAICPP enrollment, verification, PartnerIDs | Confirm vetting still shows **Authorized** and the PLA is correct for the transacting country. |
| MPA signed, CSP indirect reseller authorized | Ongoing bar is only $1,000 TTM CSP revenue + MFA on all admins + a security contact. |
| Distributor relationship live | **Do not switch distributors casually.** Change-of-Channel-Partner triggers a 12-month incentive blackout on moved subscriptions. [CHANNEL-PRESS — Crayon FY27 deck p.45] |

Things they may **not** have done, in descending order of cost of neglect:

1. **CPOR claims** — free points, and usage growth is not retroactive.
2. **Looking at the PCS dashboard at all** — the baseline nobody checks.
3. **Buying a benefits package** — most partners run the practice on customer tenants instead of an internal lab.
4. **MCI enrollment** — eligibility at 25 points + $25K TTM is widely unknown; partners assume they need the designation.
5. **Deciding whether Modern Work or Security is the faster first designation** — usually Security, because the SMB skilling table is unusually generous (3 people ≈ 16 of 25 points).

## 5. The qualification gates — the re-spec's organising idea

**Added 28 August 2026 when the field guide was re-specified to the indirect-channel lens.**

Partners do not stall on the ladder because they misunderstand badges. They stall because of **two hard resource gates that a small firm cannot conjure in a quarter**: certified headcount and paid Copilot seats. Presenting the ladder as a badge sequence hides this; presenting it against these two axes is what makes the guide honest and instantly self-locating.

| Rung | Certified people needed | Paid Copilot seats needed | Annual cash |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 0 | — | $0 |
| Claiming | 0 | — | $350–$4,125 |
| Earning (MCI) | 1–2 (for 25 capability points) | — | + exams (~$495) |
| **Designated** | **2–3** — one person can hold two of the three Security certs | ~500 MAU usage growth via CPOR | **$4,875** |
| **Specialized (M365 Copilot)** | **5 — a hard floor of five humans** | Paid Copilot MAU, threshold unpublished (old bar 1,000) | + audit ~$2.4–5K |
| Frontier | 8+ credentialed (5 Frontier Transformation Engineer badges + 3 DP-600, provisional) | High | Multi-year |

**The step change is between Designated and Specialized.** A designation is reachable at two or three certified people because certifications stack on individuals. The Copilot specialization requires **five certified individuals** — and for a five-person shop that is everyone, regardless of how capable they are. This single fact explains most SMB partner stalls at this point on the ladder, and the guide should say it plainly rather than let partners discover it after buying exam vouchers.

**The MAU gate compounds it.** If the pre-July-2026 threshold of 1,000 paid Copilot MAU carried over, that is roughly **forty SMB customers live on Copilot** at a 25-seat average deployment. The current number is unpublished, which makes it the single most planning-critical unknown in the program.

**Design consequence for the guide:** lead with self-location, not program history. A partner should be able to answer five questions — designation held, certified headcount, paid Copilot seats, CPOR filed, trailing CSP revenue — and be told both where they stand and *which specific gate is binding them*. Naming the binding constraint is more useful than naming the next badge.

## 6. POV lock: what the indirect-reseller lens changes

The re-specified guide is written from one seat and states so up front. Programs are triaged into three buckets rather than presented as a uniform ladder:

**Yours:** CSP promo spread · MCI incentives (from 25 points + $25K TTM) · co-op funds · post-sales deployment accelerators ($2,000 at a 50-seat floor) · designation benefit licences and credits · 50 TPD advisory hours · free unlimited Partner Center frontline support · the FastTrack customer benefit at 150+ seats.

**Via your distributor:** growth margins (terminate at the provider) · GCC qualification (attaches to the provider's tenant) · customer Azure support · all billing, provisioning, promotions, customer transfers and qualified-offer work.

**Not yours:** co-sell in the meaningful sense (deal registration needs a Microsoft-managed account and ≥$25K) · ECIF (field-nominated) · M365 Copilot vouchers (500-seat floor, CSP excluded) · pre-sales funded assessments (300+ seat floor) · CSP direct bill ($1M TTM) · MISA · FastTrack Ready · the Support Services designation · paid support plans, which **do not** unlock the customer-Azure path.

That last one is the sharpest piece of transparency in the guide: Microsoft's wording is that indirect resellers cannot open customer Azure requests "even if they have a support contract." The restriction is on authorization type, not entitlement, so ~$16,500/yr of Advanced Support for Partners buys nothing on the path the partner was trying to unblock. See [dossier 08](08-support-entitlements-for-indirect-resellers.md).
