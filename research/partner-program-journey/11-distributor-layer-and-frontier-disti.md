## 1. The finding that matters most

**Microsoft scores a Frontier Distributor on whether its resellers attain specializations.**

The Distributor Capability Score is **33 of 37 metrics** across six categories — GTM/Sales, Operational Readiness, Platform, Security, Support, and **Reseller Enablement** — plus a qualitative third-party audit, checked annually with the audit roughly biennial. Under **Operational Readiness**, the scored metrics include **new certifications per reseller (trailing 12 months)**, distributor technical certifications, and **reseller specialization attainment**. [COMMUNITY — corroborated independently by Alif Consulting and cloudcockpit/jamesbmarshall]

**Reseller Enablement** obliges the distributor to supply resellers with: skilling services · go-to-market resources · **practice builders** · channel insights and analytics · technical delivery capabilities. [COMMUNITY]

**Why this is leverage.** A partner pursuing the Microsoft 365 Copilot specialization is not asking their distributor for a favour — they are helping the distributor hold a designation it must renew annually. That reframes the BD conversation entirely, and it is the single most useful thing in this dossier for a reseller to know.

⚠ **Do not overstate it.** No Microsoft-authored document confirms these metrics. `partner.microsoft.com/en-us/asset/collection/frontier-distributor-collection` returns **HTTP 403** to automated fetch. Two independent consultancies agreeing is good evidence, not authoritative evidence.

## 4. Nobody sells audit preparation — the FY27 white space

Checked TD SYNNEX, Pax8, Sherweb, Ingram Micro, Crayon/SoftwareOne, Arrow. **No distributor sells specialization audit preparation.** Pax8 and Sherweb report the change in partner newsletters; neither offers a service.

**One exception, and it subsidises the fee rather than the preparation.** A **Pax8 Voyager Alliance Rewards** offer allows redemption of 10,000 points for up to **$5,000 USD** in Pax8 invoice credits against the cost of a required third-party specialization audit. Window: audits completed **2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31**; **successful attainments only**; documentation within 45 days of passing. [DISTI-OFFICIAL, weak — sourced from a Pax8-hosted PDF footer-marked "Internal"; no public landing page corroborates it]. **Verify directly with Pax8.** Note it de-risks nothing about failing.

Who *is* filling the gap: **ISSI itself** (the auditor, selling "Audit Readiness Preparation" including a rehearsal where the consultant plays auditor — see dossier 10 §7 on the conflict of interest), **The Partner Masters** (named audit-prep program covering Azure, all four Security specs, and M365 Copilot), and **AI Cloud Partners** (a $299/month evidence-tracking tool; low confidence — misexpands ISSI's name and publishes contradictory pricing).

## 5. The subcontract question — partially answered, and the answer is nuanced

### The decisive Microsoft-official fact

The Copilot specialization's performance requirement lists **Eligible Association Types: CPOR, CSP Tier 1, CSP Tier 2** [MS-OFFICIAL, specializations-apply].

**CSP Tier 2 is the indirect reseller.** So a reseller transacting Copilot seats *through TD SYNNEX* already generates eligible MAU and net-customer-add credit for its own specialization scoring — **without needing to be the party that delivered the work.** This substantially de-risks the subcontract model on the *performance* axis.

### CPOR mechanics [MS-OFFICIAL]

From learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/incentives/submit-osa-claim and incentives-customer-associations-intro:

- The partner role for Modern Work and Security is **"Use or consume"** — selected when you drive adoption/usage and want usage incentives **and/or usage/competency attainment**. That phrase is the explicit documented link from CPOR to designation scoring.
- **Proof of Execution required: a statement of work signed by the customer** (Microsoft provides a template).
- **Each workload can be associated with only one partner.** A customer may have different partners on different workloads.
- The customer is notified and has **five business days to opt out**.
- Claims cannot be edited after submission. Customer-Led channel types are not CPOR-eligible.

### What is not addressed anywhere

**Microsoft's documentation is completely silent on subcontractors.** It never states whether a partner who used a distributor or third party to deliver may claim CPOR. No distributor addresses it either — Westcon's published CPOR guide is a near-verbatim restatement of Microsoft Learn with no subcontractor guidance.

**Reasoned inference, flagged as inference:** in a white-label subcontract the reseller holds the customer SOW and the distributor is its subcontractor, so the reseller can satisfy the PoE requirement. Nothing in Microsoft's published rules contradicts this, and the "one partner per workload" rule is satisfied because the distributor does not claim. **Unverified against Microsoft policy — confirm with a PDM before building a practice on it.** [UNVERIFIED]

Distributor positioning on relationship ownership: **TD SYNNEX ServiceSolv** states it supplements partner services without competing, partners retaining the primary relationship. **Pax8** is the most explicit of any distributor — Managed Intelligence Services delivered as a white-labelled extension, and "partners maintain the client relationship and revenue." **Neither, nor any other distributor, makes any public statement about who receives Microsoft CPOR or capability-score credit.** That is the largest documented gap in this research.

### ⚠ The critical limit

**Subcontracting can get you the performance numbers. It cannot get you the audit.**

The Copilot audit tests the *partner's own* delivery maturity — its AI advisory methodology, its ROI methodology, its change-management framework, its agent lifecycle governance — plus **control 5.1, requiring 20–300 internally-deployed Copilot seats at 60% MAU concentrated in customer-facing practice and presales staff** [MS-OFFICIAL, dossier 10].

A partner that fully outsources delivery will hit the MAU and customer-add thresholds and then **fail the audit**, because it will have no first-party artefacts, no internal deployment, and no repeatable process of its own to demonstrate. **Subcontracting is a bridge to scale, not a substitute for capability.**

## 6. Market context — what "good" looks like

> ⚠ **House rule: never name competing distributors in any deliverable.** See the memory note `never-name-competitors`. The competitive comparison gathered in this research has been reduced to market-level statements below so that competitor names cannot migrate from this dossier into a partner-facing page. The repo's existing `[competitor source withheld]` convention applies here too.

**Market-level findings, stated without attribution:**

- **Attainment coaching is rare.** Only one distributor in the surveyed market publicly advertises 1:1 Partner Capability Score consultations. Most offer skilling and vouchers but stop short of coaching the score itself. TD SYNNEX's Microsoft Coaches and MAICPP Clinics sit on the stronger side of this line.
- **White-label delivery is common; scoring-credit clarity is universally absent.** Several distributors offer white-labelled delivery with the partner retaining the customer relationship. **None — including TD SYNNEX — publicly states who receives Microsoft CPOR or capability-score credit in that model.** That silence is market-wide, not a TD SYNNEX gap.
- **Nobody sells specialization audit preparation.** Across the surveyed market, no distributor offers audit-prep, evidence-pack construction, mock audits, or gap analysis against the audit controls. One distributor offers a loyalty-points redemption against the audit *fee* (successful attainments only), which de-risks nothing about failing. **This is the clearest FY27 white space in distribution.**
- **The parties actually selling audit prep** are the audit provider itself (ISSI, including a rehearsal where its consultant plays auditor — see dossier 10 §7 on the conflict of interest) and a small number of independent Microsoft partner advisory firms.
- **Frontier Distributor is held by a handful of distributors globally**, TD SYNNEX among the first, announced March 2026.

**A fair bar for what a reseller should expect** [COMMUNITY, James B. Marshall, jamesbmarshall.com/blog/choosing-a-cloud-disti/]: joint commitments on skills development, **dedicated partner-development accountability**, clear pathways toward designations *and specializations*, documented repeatable enablement with measurable ROI, and access to Microsoft-certified technical resources. His framing — hold your distributor to a higher standard and be willing to switch if the relationship is transactional rather than developmental.

**TD SYNNEX's verified position against that bar:** strongest on breadth — Practice Builder, MAICPP Clinics, Microsoft Coaches, ServiceSolv and funded certifications is the widest verified enablement stack found. The gap, shared with the whole market, is specialization audit preparation.

## 7. Questions to ask a TD SYNNEX BD rep

**On Frontier and the reseller-specialization metric**

1. Does TD SYNNEX's Frontier scorecard include a **reseller specialization attainment** metric, and where does current attainment sit against the threshold? Is there an FY27 target number of Copilot-specialized resellers?
2. Which of the 37 metrics are weakest, and where would our practice help close a gap? *(the leverage question)*
3. What does Frontier status entitle *us* to that a non-Frontier distributor's resellers don't get — anything contractual, or purely capability?
4. Do any distributor-level Frontier benefits — Azure credits, 200 internal-use licences, migration funding, support-plan discounts — pass through to resellers in any form?
5. Does EA-to-CSP for Azure transfer tool access extend to us as a Tier 2 reseller?

**On the audit white space**

6. Is there any offering — existing or roadmapped — for **Copilot specialization audit preparation**: checklist walkthrough, evidence-pack construction, mock audit, gap analysis against the 15 controls?
7. Will TD SYNNEX **fund or reimburse the ~$2,700 ISSI audit fee**, as Pax8 does via Voyager points? If not, can co-op or MDF be pointed at it?
8. Can Microsoft Coaches or MAICPP Clinics be scoped to *specialization* attainment rather than designation attainment?
9. **Control 5.1 requires 20+ internally-deployed Copilot seats at 60% MAU.** Is there an internal-use or NFR Copilot licensing program that helps a small partner meet that?
10. Who at TD SYNNEX has taken a partner through a Copilot specialization audit? Can we speak to them?

**On subcontract delivery and scoring credit**

11. If we subcontract a Copilot deployment to **ServiceSolv** under our brand, **who holds the customer SOW**, and can we use it as CPOR Proof of Execution?
12. Has TD SYNNEX confirmed with Microsoft that a reseller using ServiceSolv retains CPOR eligibility and MAU/customer-add credit? Anything in writing?
13. Does TD SYNNEX ever claim CPOR on workloads it delivers on a reseller's behalf? What policy prevents a conflicting claim?
14. Which Copilot services can ServiceSolv deliver today — readiness assessment, Purview remediation, Copilot Studio agent development? Rate card?
15. Is the **AvePoint Copilot Readiness Assessment** resold under our brand or delivered by TD SYNNEX directly? Price to us, recommended resale price?
16. What triggers **Joint Enablement + Readiness Support** funding, and how do we qualify?

**On unverified programs**

17. Do "Partner Maturity Assessment," "Copilot Campaign Framework," or "Cloud Engineering & Enablement Hub" exist internally under other names?
18. Is **Practice Builder** still actively maintained, and are the seven paths refreshed for FY27 designation and specialization requirements?
19. Is **Catalyst Partner Services** available for Copilot and agent work, or AI-vendor only? Referral or white-label — and who owns the customer?
