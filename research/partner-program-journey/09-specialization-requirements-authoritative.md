## 0. Correction notice

Dossiers 02 and 03 recorded the post-July-2026 Microsoft 365 Copilot specialization performance requirement as **"paid Copilot MAU only, threshold unpublished"** and tagged it `[UNVERIFIED]`, warning partners not to plan against the old 1,000-MAU figure.

**That was wrong.** The threshold *is* published, on a page updated 2026-08-03, and it retains **both** the 1,000 MAU growth requirement **and** the 5-net-customer requirement. The July 2026 announcement changed the *evidence* model (customer references → audit) and the *certification* pool; it did not remove the performance numbers.

Dossier 04's gate table also understated the skilling requirement as "5 certified individuals." The real requirement is **two separate groups of five**, which in practice means five people each holding two credentials.

Everything below is verbatim-sourced and should be treated as the reference of record.

## 1. Microsoft 365 Copilot specialization

### Eligibility — the multi-pathway fact

> "**Eligibility**: Active Solutions Partner designation in any one of three solution areas: **Modern Work**, **Business Applications**, or **Security**"

This is the single most important structural fact for an SMB partner. Most specializations are locked to one solution area; the Copilot specialization accepts **any one of three**. A partner who earned Security first — which the SMB skilling table makes the fastest designation to reach — has already unlocked it.

### Performance requirements

> - "Your organization must achieve a minimum of **1000 Monthly Active Usage (MAU) growth** in the following *eligible workloads* in a trailing twelve-month period. **AND**"
> - "Your organization must achieve a minimum of **5 net customer growth** in the following *eligible workloads* in a trailing twelve-month period. An eligible customer must have **at least 5 MAU**"
> - "*Eligible Workloads:* Microsoft 365 Copilot"
> - "*Eligible Association Types:* CPOR, CSP Tier 1, CSP Tier 2"
> - "**MAU** is calculated on a rolling 28-day basis and represents the unique number of users taking an intentional action on a specific service. **Your company should achieve MAU through CPOR.**"

Three mechanics worth drawing out:

1. **Both conditions, not either.** 1,000 MAU growth *and* 5 net new customers.
2. **A customer only counts if it has at least 5 MAU.** A 2-seat Copilot pilot does not count toward the 5-customer requirement. This quietly raises the floor.
3. **Microsoft explicitly directs partners to CPOR** — "Your company should achieve MAU through CPOR." This is the strongest official statement yet that CPOR, not CSP association, is the intended scoring rail, and it validates the "claim CPOR first" advice throughout this research library.

**Scale check:** 1,000 MAU growth at a 25-seat average SMB deployment is roughly **40 customers** going live on Copilot inside twelve months. At 50 seats it is 20 customers. This is the binding constraint for most SMB partners, not the certifications.

### Skilling requirements — two groups of five

> - "**5 or more** people from your company must complete at least one of the following two certifications:
>   - Microsoft Certified: Information Security Administrator Associate
>   - Microsoft Applied Skills: Prepare security and compliance to support Microsoft 365 Copilot\*
>
>   **AND**
> - **5 or more** people from your company must complete at least one of the following three:
>   - Microsoft Applied Skills: Create agents in Microsoft Copilot Studio#
>   - Agentic AI Business Solutions Architect
>   - AI Agent Builder Associate"

> \* "Prepare security and compliance to support Microsoft 365 Copilot is **retired on 30th June 2026**. If completed before retirement, it is valid for 1 year."
> \# "Create agents in Microsoft Copilot Studio is **retired on 8th July 2026**. If completed before retirement, it is valid for 1 year."

**Read this carefully.** It is **not** "five people across the pool." It is five people satisfying a security/compliance credential **and** five people satisfying an agent-building credential. The page does not require them to be different people, so the efficient shape is **five humans each holding two credentials — ten credentials total.**

Because both Applied Skills options retired in mid-2026 (and only remain valid for a year if already completed), a partner starting now is realistically looking at:

- **Group 1:** Information Security Administrator Associate (SC-401) × 5 people
- **Group 2:** Agentic AI Business Solutions Architect (AB-100) or AI Agent Builder Associate (AB-620) × 5 people

**Note on AB-100 vs AB-620.** The page lists both as satisfying group 2, so a partner can route all five through the cheaper/easier of the two. AB-620 (Associate) is the lower-altitude credential; AB-100 (Architect) is the higher one. Nothing requires a mix.

### Evidence and marketplace

> - "**Microsoft Marketplace**: N/A"
> - "**Audit or customer references**: **Audit required**"

No marketplace listing needed — a real distinction from Agentic Business Solutions, which does require one.

## 2. Data Security specialization — a much harder bar

> "**Eligibility**: Active Solutions Partner designation in the **Security** solution area"
> - "Your company must have **six or more individuals** who have passed the Microsoft Certified: **Information Security Administrator Associate** certification."
> - "AND"
> - "Your company must have **four or more individuals** who have passed any of the following applied skills:
>   - Applied Skill: Prepare security and compliance to support Copilot for Microsoft 365\* OR
>   - Applied Skill: Implement information protection and data loss prevention by using Microsoft Purview OR
>   - Applied Skill: Implement retention, eDiscovery, and Communication Compliance in Microsoft Purview"
> - "**Audit or customer references**: Audit required"

**6 + 4 credentials, minimum six humans.** This is materially harder than the Copilot specialization on headcount, and it is worth telling partners plainly: the intuition that "Data Security is the natural second specialization" is right on *practice fit* and wrong on *cost*. Sequence Copilot first.

## 3. Agentic Business Solutions — the other multi-pathway one

> "**Eligibility**: Active Solutions Partner designation in any one of three solution areas: **Digital & App Innovation**, **Business Applications**, or **Modern Work**"

Performance, one of two pathways:

> - "**Pathway one**: Two unique customers as new deployments, including at least one customer using Power Automate with a flow in production and one customer using a Power Apps application with at least five users in production. The eligible association type is PAL."
> - "**Pathway two**: Two new customer deployments of **Copilot Studio** with at least **$10,000 trailing 12-month revenue** each (nonrecurring). Eligible association types are CPOR, CSP Tier 1, and CSP Tier 2."

Skilling: "Five individuals must hold at least one of the following certifications" (single group of five, unlike Copilot).
Marketplace: "at least one consulting service offer for Power Apps, Power Automate, Copilot Studio, or Power Virtual Agents published on Microsoft Marketplace, with the corresponding product or products tagged."
Audit: **N/A**.

**Why this matters for a Copilot practice.** Modern Work also unlocks it. Pathway two needs only **two Copilot Studio customer deployments at $10K each** — no MAU threshold, no audit. For a partner building agents, this is a far cheaper specialization to reach than M365 Copilot, and the only extra cost is publishing a consulting offer to Microsoft Marketplace (which requires only a PartnerID).

## 4. The audit mechanism — verbatim

### Which specializations require an audit (17 of them)

Analytics on Microsoft Azure · AI Apps on Microsoft Azure · App Modernization on Microsoft Azure · Infra and Database migration to Microsoft Azure · Microsoft Azure Virtual Desktop · AI Platform on Microsoft Azure · Hybrid Cloud Infrastructure with Azure Stack HCI · Microsoft Azure VMware Solution · Agentic DevOps with Microsoft Azure and GitHub · Networking Services in Microsoft Azure · SAP on Microsoft Azure · **Cloud Security** · **Identity and Access Management** · **Data Security** · **Threat Protection** · **Microsoft 365 Copilot**

### How to schedule

> 1. "Sign in to Partner Center as a Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program partner admin and select **Membership**."
> 2. "Select **Specializations**, and then select *Azure*."
> 3. "Select the name of a specialization… The prerequisites for the specialization appear."
> 4. "Select **Schedule audit**."

**The gating rule, and it is the most operationally important sentence on the page:**

> "The **Schedule audit** button isn't available, and your status is **Not started**, until you meet **all** the prerequisites on the page. After you meet all the prerequisites and complete the audit, your status changes to **Active**."

**The audit is the last step, not a parallel one.** You cannot book it early, cannot run it while accumulating MAU, and cannot use it to validate work in progress. Every performance and skilling requirement must already be green.

### Failure and retry

> "**What should I do if the Audit is failed?** If your Audit is failed, you can re-initiate the Audit **after 7 days**."

A seven-day cooldown, not a quarter. This is far more forgiving than partners assume, and it pairs with the separately-announced ability (from late August 2026) to withdraw an audit in Partner Center with no cooldown before reapplying.

### Validity dates — a trap

> "**Note**: The validity of the audit date might differ from the attainment date or the validity period of the current specialization status. This distinction is important when reviewing timelines for renewal or compliance."
> "**Important**: The audit completion date used to validate certain specialization requirements might not align with the attainment date or the validity period shown for your current specialization status in Partner Center. **Always refer to the audit provider's documentation for audit-specific timelines.**"

Microsoft is explicitly disclaiming its own Partner Center dates in favour of the auditor's. Track the auditor's date, not the portal's.

## 5. Specializations still using customer references

> "Adoption and Change Management (retired – customer reference submissions are closed) · Calling for Microsoft Teams · Custom Solutions for Microsoft Teams · Meetings and Meeting Rooms for Microsoft Teams · Modernize Endpoints · **Secure AI Productivity** (formerly Teamwork Deployment)"

Reference rules, verbatim:

> "Partners must submit **three (3) customer references** for each specialization that requires customer references. Each reference must represent a **separate customer engagement**."
> A valid reference must "Be from a **named, real customer** · Be based on an engagement from the **last 24 months** · Align clearly to the **specific specialization** · Include **customer contact information** for validation · Describe the **partner solution, Microsoft technologies used, and business outcomes** · Be completed using the **official Microsoft customer reference template**."
> "Customer references are used by Microsoft **only for validation purposes** and aren't published unless both the partner and the customer explicitly agree."

**Secure AI Productivity is the notable one for this audience** — it is reference-based, not audit-based, and therefore cheaper. But note its eligibility: "Active Solutions Partner designation in **both** the **Modern Work** and **Security** solution areas" — the only specialization on the page requiring two designations.
