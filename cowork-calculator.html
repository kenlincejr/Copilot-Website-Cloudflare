What you are about to do

Two minutes of reading, then seven questions.

This works out what Cowork actually costs a specific customer — usually one you already know, with usage numbers you already have. You answer a handful of short questions, one screen at a time, with the words to say out loud printed beside every field for the ones worth confirming with them directly. At the end you get one page — the figure, the arithmetic behind it, what to sell them, and the talk track for the rest of the meeting.

$

Your pricing — set this once, before the call

Every license figure in this tool runs on the basis you pick here. Pick wrong and you will show a customer a number they are never going to pay.
1

The account

The denominator for everything else. We narrow it twice on the next two screens.
🗣
Ask out loud: “How many people work there in total — everyone, not just the ones who'd use AI?” A round number is fine.
2

Who actually holds a Copilot license

The step every other Cowork calculator skips.
This is the whole ballgame

Only someone with an assigned Microsoft 365 Copilot license can use Cowork at all. Everyone else in the company is out of scope for this number — they cost nothing here and they generate nothing. So the question is never “how many employees.” It is “how many licenses.”

Microsoft Learn — Get started with Copilot Cowork, Prerequisites: “An active Microsoft 365 Copilot license assigned to your account.” The SKUs and what each one includes are in section 6, the Licensing Snapshot.

🗣
Ask out loud: “Of those people, how many have a Copilot license today?” If the answer is “none yet,” ask the follow-up: “If we started with one team, which team, and how many people is that?”
3

Who actually turns it on

Having the license isn't using it. Only active users burn credits.
🗣
Ask out loud: “Realistically, in the first ninety days — is this the whole group, or a first team?” Whatever they say is your active number.
4

Who they are — and how often they'd use it

Two things on this screen: which kinds of people would use Cowork, and how many tasks each of them runs in a month. What a task costs comes next.
🗣
Ask out loud: “Of the people who'd really use it — roughly how many are desk-and-document people, how many are in front of customers, how many are technical, and how many are managers?” Round numbers. “Ten, eight, four, four” is a perfectly good answer.
🎭 What's a “persona,” exactly?

Not a job title — just a rough bucket for how someone spends most of their day.

Corporate Knowledge WorkerDay-to-day office work — email, documents, decks, meetings.
Customer-FacingSales, support, account management.
TechnicalIT, developers, data and analytics roles.
Manager / Senior LeaderOversight and reporting more than hands-on execution.
⚙ What makes a task “light,” “medium,” or “heavy”?

Think in terms of how long the task would take a person, not how it feels to type the prompt.

LIGHT · under ~10 min
“Summarize this document,” draft a short email, pull one answer from SharePoint.
MEDIUM · ~30–60 min
Research and draft a report, reconcile a spreadsheet, build a first-pass deck.
HEAVY · multi-hour
A genuinely multi-step, multi-tool project. The kind of thing you'd block an afternoon for.
Which of these is closest?
These describe the licensed, active population — not the whole company. In most of them the field, floor, store or ward sits on a frontline SKU and never reaches this table.
5

What a task costs — and how hard Cowork works

Step 4 counted the tasks. This screen puts a credit price on each one, and sets the effort level that scales all of them at once.
🗣
Ask out loud: “When you picture someone actually using this — is it mostly quick jobs, or the big multi-hour ones?” You are calibrating, not auditing. And if anyone there has already run a real task, ask them to type /cost and read you the number — that beats every default on this screen.
🎯 Don't guess. Measure.

If the customer — or you, running Cowork internally — already has tasks running, open a task in Cowork chat and type /cost. Enter what three real tasks actually cost and this estimate stops being a model.

Two things to know: /cost shows only what a task has already used — Microsoft doesn't support checking before you run — and it is an in-product approximation, not the billing record. Microsoft Learn — Check credit usage with /cost.

6

How they'll buy the credits

The billing model doesn't change consumption — it changes the unit price.
🗣
Ask out loud: “Would you rather pay for this monthly as you use it, or pre-buy a block up front for a discount?” For a first year the honest answer is almost always start metered — they have no baseline yet, and P3 purchases are final.
7

Governance readiness

Every answer here moves the licensing recommendation. Nothing is decorative.
🗣
Ask out loud: “Has IT already turned on anything like sensitivity labels or data-loss protection for AI tools?” You're not auditing anything on the spot — you just want to know what to check first.
