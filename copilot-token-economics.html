Your customer did not ask for a meter. They bought Copilot the way they buy everything from Microsoft: a price per person, per month, that finance can put in a budget. 3.1 · Why a ceiling nobody watches becomes the bill

The honest answer to “will they really spend $40?” is: some will, most will not, and nobody in the customer's building has the job of finding out which. Three things make that worse than it sounds.

  • New metered services switch themselves on. In the admin center, spending policies auto-apply to new services by default, and usage-based features are visible to all users by default. The ceiling grows on its own.
  • The heavy users are real. Microsoft's own Cowork estimator put an engaged user at $82 to $228 a month in credits. On the Cowork simulator's stock defaults, a modeled user hits the 4,000-credit ceiling around day 8 of the month. The ceiling is not theoretical for the people who get the most from the product.
  • The bill arrives on your Azure plan. Microsoft's CSP guidance tells you to link an Azure subscription to your partner billing account before usage billing starts. You carry the collection risk on every credit the customer burns, whether or not you are charging for governance.

3.2 · What a credit actually buys

Partners lose this conversation by making it complicated. There are three kinds of Copilot work, and three price tags. A credit is one cent. Microsoft publishes no conversion from credits to tokens, and you should never offer one.

$0

Chat in Word, Outlook, Teams and the Copilot app. A licensed user talking to a standard Copilot Studio agent inside Microsoft channels, under their own identity. No meter.

On the meter, counted in dollars

$82 to $228

Telling Copilot to go do a job

Cowork, Code and Autopilot. “Build the board pack from these six folders.” Microsoft's own estimator range for an engaged user, per month, if nobody is watching.

The zero-rating rule is the first question, not the last

A licensed user's own use of a standard agent is free with the seat. The bill comes from Cowork, Code, Autopilot, unlicensed and external users, autonomous runs, computer use and the GitHub harness. A partner who cannot draw that line for every agent before building it will over-quote the deal and lose it, or under-quote it and eat the difference. 4.2 · The agent meter, in five facts

  • Free only when four things hold. A licensed user, signed in with their own Microsoft identity, in Copilot Chat, Teams or SharePoint, on the standard or chat harness. Anything else bills: website, WhatsApp, SMS, voice, guests, unlicensed staff, autonomous triggers, computer use.
  • No default limit. Microsoft's own guidance: allocate a fixed amount per environment, turn off tenant draw, and set a limit per agent.
  • The hard stop turns the agent off. It protects the bill and breaks the service. Set the alert threshold well below the stop, and name who decides whether to raise it.
  • Building costs credits now. GitHub-harness agents bill from the first build action, in developer and trial environments too. Put a build budget in the statement of work.
  • Estimates run low. Turning on reasoning or tenant grounding after launch multiplies the credits per answer. Freeze those settings in the agent's scope.

4.3 · The business-app meter

Your Business Central and Power Automate customers are on the meter without a Copilot seat

Business Central's Payables agent is 50 credits an invoice plus 5 a line, and the Expense agent is 50 a receipt. Neither is covered by the Copilot included in Business Central. They draw from the default Power Platform environment unless you relink them. Separately, AI Builder credits can no longer be bought or renewed after November 1, 2026, and new premium licenses stop including them. When a customer's AI Builder credits run out, Power Automate's AI steps draw Copilot Credits, or stop if none are available. Every Business Central and Power Automate customer is heading onto the meter whether or not they bought Copilot.

4.4 · The developer meter

  • Since June 1, 2026, GitHub bills Copilot usage in AI credits. A credit is a penny. Copilot Business includes 1,900 a license a month and costs $19; Enterprise includes 3,900 and costs $39. The allowance pools across the organization.
  • Paid usage is on by default. GitHub's words: “Additional usage is enabled by default.” No budget is set for you.
  • When a budget runs out, work stops. “There is no automatic fallback to lower-cost models.” A developer hits a wall mid-release.
  • The controls exist: user budgets, cost-center budgets, enterprise spending limits, alerts at 75, 90 and 100%, a switch that stops paid usage, and in-product requests for more.
  • It is not on your invoice. GitHub's Azure billing does not support the CSP Azure plan, so the customer pays GitHub directly. That is why nobody watches it, and why the service is governance, not resale.
  • Two GitHub meters, not one. The GitHub harness inside Copilot Studio bills Copilot Credits on your invoice. GitHub Copilot for developers bills GitHub credits on theirs.

7 · The Service in Four Verbs

This is the whole managed service. Everything else in the playbook, Tier 1 to 3, AgentCare, the Agent 365 overlay, wraps around it. It maps directly onto the FinOps Foundation's Inform, Optimize, Operate loop, and onto the controls Microsoft already shipped in the Microsoft 365 admin center under Copilot, then Cost management. The verbs are the same on every meter. Only the admin screen changes.

SET

Onboarding, then quarterly

Agree a credit budget per tenant and per group. Lower the 4,000 default to the agreed limit. Alerts at 80% and 100%. Decide which services are allowed. Pick the funding source.

WATCH

Monthly, 1 to 2 hours a tenant

Read the meter. Top consumers, idle agents, spikes, users hitting limits, requests for more. Baseline for 30 to 60 days before you move a threshold.

TUNE

Monthly, re-plan quarterly

Steady users onto prepaid packs. Pre-purchase only at break-even. Retire idle agents. Route routine work to the cheaper model. Remove unused seats.

PROVE

One page, every month

Credits spent, by whom, on what, next to the outcome. Cost per user per month. Cost per resolved request for each agent. It feeds the QBR.

SETTurn the default into a decisiononboarding · 30 min in the admin center, with the customer on the call
You do
  • Confirm the Azure subscription is linked to your partner billing account, so the meter is wired before the first credit burns.
  • Create the spending policy. Replace the 4,000-credit default with the limit the customer chose. Set a tenant-level limit too.
  • Switch on alerts at 80% and 100% of budget. They repeat daily until reset, so route them to a mailbox someone reads.
  • Review auto-apply for new services. Decide, with the customer, whether a new metered service is on by default or off until approved.
  • Name the budget owner on the customer side. One person. Write it in the policy description.

9 · Pricing Every Meter

9.1 · Where the playbook is today, and why it moves

Credit Wrap in Model the Practice is defined as an Azure consumption markup: buy pooled credits at CSP cost, bill at a 30% markup, earn roughly $75 to $150 a month per customer, “modest at Tier 2.” That definition was written when consumption was a side effect of a few Copilot Studio agents. Three things changed under it.

  1. Consumption is now attached to every Copilot Business seat, not just agent customers. Credit Wrap belongs at Tier 1, next to the oversharing assessment, not at Tier 2.
  2. Pure percentage pricing fails at SMB scale. Thirty percent of a $250 meter is $75. Thirty percent of a tenant that stays under its budget is nothing. Published norms for spend-based fees in adjacent markets run 1 to 5% of spend, and nobody publishes a Copilot markup. The revenue has to sit in the floor, not the markup.
  3. Microsoft routes the bill through you, so you already carry the collection risk whether or not you charge for governance.

AgentCare in Model the Practice charges $350 an agent at Tier 2 and $400 at Tier 3, one rate for every agent. We went looking for a market price to test it against and found none: no partner, analyst or forum publishes a monthly price per agent. The nearest anchors put one agent at $350 below every recurring floor the market shows, and one rate misprices both ends. An internal FAQ agent costs nothing to run and needs an hour a month. A website agent carries billed traffic, brand risk and a weekly review.

9.2 · What the market charges

The evidence is the market table in section 5: builds and governance retainers are priced, and the monthly price per agent or per developer is not. Gartner, Forrester and IDC publish no per-agent or per-developer benchmark. Their message is direction: spending moves from seats to usage and outcomes.

16 · Guardrails: What Never To Say

Trap 1

“A credit is about N tokens.”

Microsoft publishes no conversion, and per-task consumption varies by model, context and tools. An automated summary of the Credits Guide invented one; the actual document has none. The credit is the unit. Stop there.

Trap 2

“The default limit is $10 a user.”

One trade publication reported a $10 preset. Microsoft's own October 1 announcement says 4,000 credits, which is $40. Use Microsoft's figure, always.

Trap 3

“Pre-purchase will save you 20%.”

Only the largest tier reaches 20%, the smallest is 5%, unused credits expire, and the FinOps rule is a 60 to 90 day baseline before any commitment. Say “we will tell you when prepaying pays,” and mean it.

Trap 4

“We'll keep your AI bill down.”

That pitch fights Microsoft's incentives, which pay you on sustained consumption, and it fights the customer's reason for buying. Say “we'll make sure you spend it on purpose.” See section 11.

Trap 5

“Microsoft told partners to build this service.”

It did not, in so many words. It named FinOps for AI and managed services as partner opportunities, built the controls, set the default and routed the bill through you. Say that, exactly, and let the partner draw the conclusion.

Trap 6

Naming two products as if they were one

The Credits Guide says Microsoft 365 Copilot became Microsoft Copilot in August 2026. Partner Center still writes “Microsoft 365 Copilot Business.” Both names are live. Use the one on the document in front of the customer.

Trap 7

“Agents are free with Copilot.”

Only when all four tests hold. Say “most internal agents are free; anything facing customers or running alone is metered.”

Trap 8

“GitHub credits and Copilot Credits are the same.”

Same penny, different meter, different invoice. The GitHub harness in Copilot Studio is Copilot Credits; GitHub Copilot is GitHub credits.

Trap 9

“We'll put a hard stop on it and you're covered.”

The stop turns the agent off. Alert first, decide, then stop.

Trap 10

“Every agent is $350 a month to manage.”

One rate overcharges the internal agent and undercharges the website agent. Price by who it talks to.

Trap 11

“Your AI Builder credits disappear on November 1.”

Microsoft's page says both that seeded credits are removed on November 1 and that they are honored to the end of the contract term. Say what is certain: no new or renewed AI Builder credits after November 1.
